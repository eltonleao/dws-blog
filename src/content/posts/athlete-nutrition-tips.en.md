# Athlete Nutrition Tips

Athletes need more than a healthy diet; they need food that matches the work they ask of their bodies. Good sports nutrition comes down to fuel and timing: having energy ready before training, replacing what you burn during long efforts and giving muscles the raw material to repair afterward.

Carbohydrate is the main fuel for hard exercise. The body stores it as glycogen in the muscles and liver, and those stores are limited: a long run or a tough match can drain them, and when they run low, pace drops and every effort feels heavier. Fat fuels easy work well, but it cannot be burned fast enough for high intensity. That is why athletes who cut carbohydrate too far often feel flat in the sessions that matter most.

Plan your meals around the training day. A few hours before a hard session, eat a familiar meal built on easily digested carbohydrate with a little protein, keeping fat and fiber modest so your stomach stays calm. During long efforts, sip a sports drink or eat simple snacks such as bananas or gels, and practice this in training so your gut learns to cope. Afterward, a meal that combines carbohydrate and protein starts refilling glycogen and repairing muscle.

Protein works best spread across the day, since muscle repair continues for hours after training. Include a portion at every meal, from eggs, dairy, fish, meat, legumes or tofu. Just as important is eating enough overall. When intake falls short of what training demands, the body saves energy by slowing recovery and hormone production, bringing fatigue, frequent colds, stress fractures and stalled progress. Match portions to the load: more on hard days, less on rest days.

Hydration deserves a plan, too. Weigh yourself before and after a session in the heat: the weight you lose is mostly sweat, so replace it gradually with water and a little salt. If your clothes dry with white streaks, you may need extra sodium. Treat supplements with caution, since few have solid evidence behind them and some are contaminated with banned substances. Put food first, and try every change in training, never on race day.
