# Best Road Trips in Europe

Europe packs an astonishing range of landscapes into short drives, from sea cliffs and fjords to alpine passes and sunbaked hill towns. A road trip lets you stop wherever the view demands, and the best routes reward a slow pace, a small car and a little homework on local driving rules.

In the Scottish Highlands, roads wind through empty glens, past lochs and down to a coast scattered with islands. Many are single-track, with passing places to let oncoming cars by, so drive slowly and wave your thanks. The west of Ireland offers a similar mood, with green headlands, stone walls and harbor villages. In both you drive on the left, and the weather changes by the hour.

Norway's fjord country is a road trip built around water. Roads climb in hairpins over mountain plateaus and drop to deep inlets, where ferry crossings are part of the route. In the Alps, high passes lead from valley to valley past glaciers, meadows and wooden villages. Many close in winter and some open only in early summer, so check ahead, and use a low gear on the way down to spare your brakes.

The Mediterranean offers a warmer kind of drive. The coastal road along the cliffs of southern Italy clings to the rock above lemon groves and fishing towns; go early, in a compact car, before the tour buses arrive. Inland Andalusia strings white villages across olive country, while the Dalmatian coast of Croatia threads between the sea and bare limestone mountains. In summer, drive in the morning and spend hot afternoons by the water.

A little preparation keeps the trip smooth. Some motorways charge tolls at booths, while others require a sticker or digital pass bought before you enter. Many city centers restrict older or polluting cars, so park outside and walk into old towns. Rental cars are often manual unless you ask otherwise, and crossing a border may need the agency's approval. Carry your license, insurance papers and the safety kit some countries require, such as a warning triangle and reflective vest. Then leave gaps in the schedule, because the best stops are rarely the planned ones.
