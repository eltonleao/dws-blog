# Dressing Up for Winter

Dressing well for winter means staying warm without looking wrapped in a sleeping bag. The secret is to think in layers, each with its own job, and to choose a few good pieces that keep heat in, keep wind and water out, and still look sharp at the office or at dinner.

Clothing does not make heat; your body does. A garment traps still air close to the skin, and still air conducts heat poorly. That is why several thin layers often beat one thick sweater: each holds its own pocket of air, and you can take one off indoors. Moisture ruins that trapped air. Sweat soaks into cotton and pulls heat away, so the layer against your skin should be wool or a fabric that wicks moisture outward.

Build the outfit from the inside out. A fine merino or silk base layer disappears under a shirt and adds real warmth. Over it, a knit in wool or cashmere insulates without spoiling the line, and a fine turtleneck works under a blazer as well as under a coat. The outer layer must block the wind, since moving air strips away that warm pocket. A wool overcoat that covers your hips does that job and dresses up anything beneath it, from jeans to a suit.

Hands, feet and head lose heat quickly because they have little muscle and plenty of exposed surface. Leather gloves with a soft lining, a solid scarf and a knit hat can be the most elegant part of the outfit, not an afterthought. Choose leather boots with a thick sole that keeps you off the cold ground, and waterproof them before the first snow. Wool socks stay warm even when a little damp.

Winter suits a restrained palette. Camel, charcoal, navy and deep green mix easily, so a few pieces give many combinations, and texture does the work that bright color does in summer: tweed next to fine knit, suede next to smooth leather. Before buying, check the fiber label and the seams, and try the coat on over a sweater. A well chosen layer, brushed and rested between wears, will serve you for many winters.
