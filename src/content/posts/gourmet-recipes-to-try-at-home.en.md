# Gourmet Recipes to Try at Home

Gourmet cooking at home depends less on rare ingredients than on a few restaurant techniques that anyone can learn. Master a proper sear, a glossy pan sauce, a creamy risotto and a light chocolate mousse, and you have a menu that feels special on a weeknight and impressive at a dinner party.

Start with the sear, because that is where most of the flavor comes from. When meat or fish hits a very hot pan, sugars and proteins on the surface react and form a brown crust full of roasted, savory notes. That reaction needs a dry surface, so pat the steak dry and salt it ahead of time. Lay it in a heavy pan with shimmering oil and leave it alone until it releases on its own, then let it rest before slicing.

The brown bits left in the pan are the base of a classic sauce. Pour off most of the fat, soften a sliced shallot, add a splash of wine or stock and scrape the bottom as it bubbles. Once it thickens slightly, take the pan off the heat and swirl in a knob of cold butter. Cold butter melts slowly and stays suspended in the liquid, which gives the sauce its velvety shine.

Risotto rewards patience. Toast short-grain rice in butter with onion, then add warm stock a ladle at a time, stirring often. The stirring rubs starch off each grain, and that starch turns the liquid creamy without any cream. The rice is ready when it is tender with a slight bite in the center. Fold in grated hard cheese and serve at once, loose enough to spread on the plate.

For dessert, melt dark chocolate gently, let it cool until just warm and fold in softly whipped cream in a few additions, turning the bowl instead of stirring. The air you keep is the texture you get. Whatever the dish, read the recipe through before starting, set out everything you need and taste before serving. Gourmet is mostly attention, applied one step at a time.
