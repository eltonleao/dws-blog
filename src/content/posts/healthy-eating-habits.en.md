# Healthy Eating Habits

Healthy eating is less about strict rules and more about habits that make good choices the easy ones. A few steady routines, repeated most days, do more for your energy, weight and long-term health than any short diet, and they leave room for the foods you enjoy.

Start with how food keeps you full. Protein, fiber and water add volume and slow digestion, so a meal built around them keeps hunger quiet for hours. Refined starches and sugary drinks are absorbed quickly, which raises blood sugar fast and lets it fall just as fast, often followed by cravings. That is why a plate of vegetables, beans and fish satisfies longer than a pastry with the same amount of calories.

A simple way to put this into practice is to build the plate instead of counting. Fill half of it with vegetables or fruit, a quarter with a protein such as eggs, legumes, fish or chicken, and the rest with whole grains or potatoes. Add a little olive oil, nuts or seeds for healthy fats. The method works at home, at a restaurant and in a packed lunch, and it needs no app.

Habits depend on your surroundings more than on willpower. Keep fruit in plain sight and put sweets and cookies away. Plan a few dinners before you shop, and cook extra so tomorrow's lunch is already done. Read labels for added sugar and salt, which hide in sauces, breads and breakfast cereals. When a craving hits, drink water first, since thirst is easy to confuse with hunger.

Finally, slow down. The signal that you have eaten enough takes a while to reach the brain, so eating quickly makes it easy to overshoot. Sit at a table, put the phone away and pause halfway through the meal. Leave room for flexibility, too: a dessert at a birthday or a big holiday meal does not undo a week of good choices. Consistency, not perfection, is what changes your health over time.
