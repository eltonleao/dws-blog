# How to Style Your Wardrobe

Styling is the art of making the clothes you already own work harder. Before buying anything new, learn what suits you, how pieces work together and which details turn a plain outfit into an intentional one. The result is a closet with fewer clothes and more good days.

Start with an honest edit. Try everything on and sort it into three piles: what you love and wear, what needs repair or tailoring, and what no longer fits your body or your life. Then look for patterns in the first pile. Most people return to the same colors, fabrics and shapes again and again, and those favorites reveal your real style better than any magazine.

Good outfits rely on proportion. The eye reads a silhouette as a whole, so balance volume in one place with structure in another: a loose sweater over slim trousers, or a fitted top with wide leg pants. Defining the waist, by tucking in a shirt or adding a belt, breaks a long shape into pleasing parts. Fit matters more than price, and a simple hem or a nip at the waist from a tailor can make an inexpensive piece look custom made.

Color and texture tie a wardrobe together. Choose a base of neutrals that all mix, such as navy, gray, cream or olive, then add accents that flatter your skin and hair. When an outfit feels flat, change the texture instead of adding more color: chunky knit with denim, silk with wool, matte with shine. A third layer, like a jacket, a cardigan or a scarf, turns a simple top and trousers into a finished look. Small gestures count too: roll the sleeves or half tuck a shirt.

Finally, make styling easy on busy mornings. Write down a few outfit formulas, such as dark jeans, a crisp white shirt and loafers, and repeat them with different pieces. Photograph combinations you love so you can find them again. Keep a short list of what is truly missing and shop from it, not on impulse. Hang clothes where you can see them, and bring a forgotten piece to the front each week.
